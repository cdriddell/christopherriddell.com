About

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at the Child Development and Education Research Institute at the University of Amsterdam. My research examines children’s socio-emotional development, particularly how children express their own and understand others’ emotions.

I investigate how components of emotion—including facial expressions, body language, and physiological change—shape social behaviour and development. A central interest is in self-conscious emotions, including embarrassment and pride, and in the role of early parent–child emotional interactions in their emergence.

More recently, I have expanded this work to cross-cultural and comparative contexts, investigating how emotions are expressed and perceived across societies, developmental stages, and nonhuman animals.

Research

My work combines developmental, social, and comparative approaches to examine emotion as an embodied and relational process.

  • Self-conscious emotionsThe developmental emergence of embarrassment, pride, and related social-emotional expressions.
  • Parent–child interactionHow synchrony, mind-mindedness, and emotional socialisation relate to children’s emotional development.
  • Emotion perceptionAge-related, cross-cultural, and multimodal changes in recognising and producing emotion.
  • Comparative emotion scienceWhat nonhuman primates and human development can reveal about emotion and social behaviour.
